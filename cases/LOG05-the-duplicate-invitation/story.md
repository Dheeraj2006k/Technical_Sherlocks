# Hook

At the QUBIT Innovation Showcase, Team Orbit nearly lost its first prototype-demo slot after arriving at registration with an invitation that said **4:30 PM**. The signed master run sheet and the official email attachment both say **4:00 PM**. The QR code on the invitation is valid, which makes the mismatch more confusing: the code points to Orbit, but the time is wrong.

No one was disqualified, and the demo schedule was paused while the organisers checked the records. A duplicate-looking invitation was later found in the paper-recycling bin. Your job is to determine who deliberately made the misleading copy and why.

Do not assume the person who had access to the printer must be responsible. Distinguish an account log from a person, a valid QR from an authentic invitation, and a genuine schedule edit from a changed paper copy.

## Phase 1 briefing

Phase 1, The Mismatch. The invitation Team Orbit received, the signed master run sheet, the check-in desk note and the volunteer shift board are open. Establish the contradiction first, and be careful not to add a cause the records do not give you.

## Phase 2 briefing

Phase 2, The Duplicate. The official email archive, the print queue receipt, the duplicate-QR audit and the printer tray photograph are open. Work out what was officially sent and what each technical record can and cannot prove.

## Phase 3 briefing

Phase 3, The Motive. The rejected proof, a handwritten shift note, the schedule revision history and Team Nova's receipt are open. Bring the physical and written evidence together with the earlier records.
